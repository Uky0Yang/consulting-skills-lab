# Consulting Slide Quality Checklist

Run this after rendering every slide.

## Narrative

- [ ] The slide has one clear narrative job.
- [ ] The title states a claim or implication.
- [ ] The proof object supports the title.
- [ ] The implication changes a decision, priority, or action.
- [ ] The page advances the deck rather than repeating an earlier point.

## Style

- [ ] One house style is applied consistently.
- [ ] Typography matches the selected style.
- [ ] Accent color identifies the answer rather than decorating the page.
- [ ] Grey or pale tints carry context and secondary information.
- [ ] Titles, rules, footers, and page numbers align across slides.

## Charts and Tables

- [ ] Units, time periods, and definitions are visible.
- [ ] Data labels and totals reconcile.
- [ ] The focal series, row, or scenario is unmistakable.
- [ ] Legends are removed when direct labels are clearer.
- [ ] Gridlines, borders, and fills are no heavier than necessary.
- [ ] Sources and material caveats are present.

## Layout

- [ ] No unintended overlaps, clipping, or objects outside the slide.
- [ ] Titles do not wrap unexpectedly.
- [ ] Body text remains legible at normal viewing size.
- [ ] Objects align to a visible grid.
- [ ] Whitespace separates logical groups.
- [ ] The page still communicates at thumbnail size.

## PowerPoint Structure

- [ ] No unresolved placeholders remain.
- [ ] Masters and layouts are not damaged.
- [ ] Page markers and footers are intentional.
- [ ] Charts, tables, and text remain editable when required.
- [ ] No hidden source-deck content or client data remains.

## Rights and Representation

- [ ] The deck is not described as an official firm template.
- [ ] No unauthorized firm logo or proprietary source slide is included.
- [ ] No copyrighted chart, wording, or screenshot was copied without permission.
- [ ] Any client branding was supplied or authorized by the user.

## Final Gate

Reject the slide if any answer below is no:

1. Can the audience state the claim after five seconds?
2. Can they identify the evidence after ten seconds?
3. Can they tell what matters most without reading every label?
4. Does the page remain credible without the accent color?
5. Is every visible object earning its space?
