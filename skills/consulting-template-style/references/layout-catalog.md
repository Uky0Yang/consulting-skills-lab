# Consulting Layout Catalog

Choose a layout based on the slide's narrative job, not because a template slot is available.

## Core Archetypes

| Archetype | Narrative job | Primary structure | Suitable styles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal cover | Establish topic and context | Title, subtitle, date, restrained visual field | McKinsey, BCG |
| Executive synthesis | State the answer and supporting logic | Claim, 3-4 reasons, implication | All |
| Chart plus commentary | Prove one quantified claim | 65-75% chart, 25-35% interpretation | Bain, BCG |
| Two-column argument | Compare logic and evidence | Drivers left, proof right | All |
| Full-width trend | Show change over time | Direct-labeled chart, bottom implication | Bain, BCG |
| Waterfall or bridge | Explain movement between two states | Start, drivers, end, annotated delta | All |
| Matrix or scatter | Prioritize or position options | Two axes, highlighted zone, direct labels | Bain, BCG |
| Dense comparison table | Make a choice across criteria | Compact rows, one selected option | Bain, BCG |
| Process or roadmap | Explain sequence and ownership | Horizontal stages, gates, milestones | BCG |
| Market map | Show structure or competitive space | Segments, players, white space | Bain, BCG |
| Decision tree | Show choices and conditions | Branches, criteria, recommendation | McKinsey, BCG |
| Section divider | Signal a narrative pivot | Short claim or section label | All |

## Composition Rules

### Minimal cover

- Use one title block and at most one supporting visual gesture.
- Keep logos out unless the user supplies authorized assets.
- Do not place agenda details on the cover.

### Executive synthesis

- Lead with the recommendation.
- Use evidence-backed reasons, not generic themes.
- End with the decision, risk, or action required.
- McKinsey-informed pages can use whitespace; Bain-informed pages can use a compact evidence strip.

### Chart plus commentary

- The chart carries the proof.
- The commentary names the observation, implication, and action.
- Do not repeat every chart label in prose.
- Use one accented series, bar, or period.

### Two-column argument

- Maintain a clear relationship between columns: cause/effect, option/evidence, issue/proof, or current/future.
- Do not use two unrelated content panels.
- Align column headers, baselines, and bottom edges.

### Full-width trend

- Use direct labels where practical.
- Remove unnecessary gridlines and legends.
- Annotate the inflection point or decision-relevant period.
- Reserve the bottom strip for the implication, not another summary.

### Waterfall or bridge

- Order drivers logically.
- Label units and period.
- Distinguish positive and negative drivers only when analytically necessary.
- Accent the end state or the most important driver.

### Matrix or scatter

- Give each axis a decision-relevant meaning.
- Label the desirable direction.
- Highlight only the selected zone or focal point.
- Avoid decorative quadrants with no underlying logic.

### Dense comparison table

- Keep borders light.
- Use row hierarchy and whitespace before adding fills.
- Highlight one row, column, or decision criterion.
- Put the conclusion in the title or adjacent implication box.

### Process or roadmap

- Use a horizontal flow when sequence matters.
- Show gates, owners, or decision points explicitly.
- Use tint bands for phases and strong accent only for the current or recommended path.
- Avoid disconnected rounded cards.

### Market map

- Define the organizing dimensions before placing companies or segments.
- Separate factual placement from inferred placement.
- Use size, color, or shape only when the encoding adds meaning.
- State the white-space implication.

## Density Budget

| Density | Use when | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Senior recommendation or cover | One proof object, 2-4 supporting points |
| Medium | Strategy, process, or decision page | One main object plus one secondary explanation |
| High | Diligence, benchmark, or chart book | Multiple aligned objects with strict grid and legible labels |

If content exceeds the density budget, split the slide. Do not shrink until labels become difficult to read.
