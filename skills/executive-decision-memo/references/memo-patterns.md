# Memo Patterns

Use these patterns when the output must be board-ready, executive-ready, or decision-ready.

## One-Page Decision Memo

Best when the audience already knows the context.

```markdown
## Recommendation
<Direct answer in one sentence>

## Why Now
<Decision trigger and consequence of delay>

## Rationale
1. <Reason with evidence>
2. <Reason with evidence>
3. <Reason with evidence>

## Tradeoff
<The real downside or opportunity cost>

## Decision Needed
<Approval, budget, scope, timing, or owner>
```

## Board Memo

Best when oversight, risk, capital, or strategic direction is involved.

Sections:

1. Decision requested.
2. Strategic context.
3. Recommendation.
4. Options considered.
5. Financial, operational, and risk implications.
6. Management action plan.
7. Board discussion questions.

## Investment Committee Memo

Best for investment or resource allocation decisions.

Sections:

1. Recommendation and ask.
2. Thesis.
3. Market and strategic rationale.
4. Economics and sensitivity.
5. Risks and mitigations.
6. Diligence gaps.
7. Decision terms or conditions.

## Steering Committee Memo

Best for transformation programs.

Sections:

1. Program status.
2. Decisions needed.
3. Benefits and milestones.
4. Key risks and blockers.
5. Required executive actions.
6. Next checkpoint.

## Title Standards

Weak:

- "Update on AI Program"
- "Market Analysis Summary"

Stronger:

- "Approve 90-Day Scale Plan for Two AI Workflows"
- "Continue Diligence, but Reprice Unless Retention Data Improves"

## Risk Language

Weak risk language hides the decision:

- "There may be execution risk."

Decision-ready risk language connects risk to action:

- "The main execution risk is manager adoption. Mitigate by requiring each region leader to nominate one workflow owner before rollout; pause expansion if adoption stays below 60% after four weeks."
