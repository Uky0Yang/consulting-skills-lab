# Framing methods

These are original working heuristics, not proprietary consulting-firm standards.

## Choose the tree by decision

- Profit: revenue minus variable and fixed costs; split revenue into volume, realized price and mix. Reconcile totals before attributing changes.
- Conversion: define the eligible cohort and funnel stages. A shrinking denominator can improve the rate without improving outcomes.
- Operations: demand, effective capacity and flow losses; distinguish throughput from utilization and waiting time.
- A strategic choice: compare customer value, economics and feasibility across genuine alternatives rather than forcing an accounting tree.

Label a tree's scope and level. Avoid mixing a driver (conversion), a cause (slow onboarding) and an intervention (automation) as siblings. Investigate unexplained residuals instead of distributing them across favored explanations.

## Reframe without changing the mandate

Use: “The requested analysis is X. Evidence E also suggests Y may affect the decision. I propose testing Y within boundary B; please confirm before the project scope changes.” If X is the user's fixed requirement, retain it and document the tradeoff.

For an unmeasured target, write “target to agree” and specify a data request. A provisional numerical scenario must be labelled illustrative and cannot masquerade as the sponsor's objective.

## Rival-hypothesis test

For “onboarding drives churn,” compare renewal behavior within similar size/contract cohorts, not just overall churn. Inspect cancellation reasons, product activation and service incidents. Ask what pattern would contradict the hypothesis. State cohort selection, confounders and limits; the comparison is not automatically a causal experiment.

## Work selection and stop rules

Select the test that can change the imminent choice, not the largest data exercise. Example: if a funding decision depends on whether a margin decline is price or cost driven, reconcile the margin bridge before commissioning an entire market study.

Use decision-specific thresholds agreed with the sponsor. Separate “stop the analysis because evidence is unavailable” from “reject the option because a criterion failed.” Escalate a scope conflict; do not manufacture certainty to meet a deadline.
