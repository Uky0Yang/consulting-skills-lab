# Commercial Diligence Red Flags

Use this reference when the user needs risks, deal breakers, or thesis invalidation tests.

## Market Red Flags

- Growth depends on one temporary policy, channel, subsidy, or platform rule.
- The attractive segment is much smaller than the headline market.
- Profit pools are shifting away from the target's position in the value chain.
- Demand is cyclical but projections assume a structural growth curve.

## Customer Red Flags

- High concentration with weak contractual protection.
- Low willingness to recommend or repurchase.
- Switching costs are operationally low despite management claims.
- Growth is driven by discounting, one-time projects, or channel stuffing.
- Customer references are curated and do not represent the core base.

## Competitive Red Flags

- Differentiation is easy to copy.
- A larger competitor controls distribution, data, or ecosystem access.
- Substitutes are improving faster than direct competitors.
- Target wins on price but claims premium positioning.

## Commercial Metric Red Flags

- Retention, churn, or cohort data is unavailable.
- Reported recurring revenue includes non-recurring services.
- Pipeline quality is weak or stage definitions changed recently.
- Sales productivity depends on one founder, region, or channel partner.
- Forecast accuracy has been poor but projections are aggressive.

## Execution Red Flags

- Value creation plan depends on capabilities the buyer does not have.
- Integration requirements are understated.
- Management team cannot explain unit economics or segment economics.
- Regulatory or data risks are treated as legal footnotes rather than commercial constraints.

## Kill Criteria

Define deal-specific kill criteria before analysis starts. Examples:

- Market growth is below the threshold required for the underwriting case.
- Retention or churn invalidates the recurring revenue thesis.
- Customer concentration risk cannot be mitigated in terms or price.
- Product differentiation is not confirmed by customers or win/loss evidence.
- Value creation plan requires unrealistic execution speed.
