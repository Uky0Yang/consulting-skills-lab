# Commercial Due Diligence Sprint — Worked Example

## User prompt

> Use $commercial-due-diligence-sprint to frame a ten-day diligence of a £70m-revenue vertical SaaS target serving UK dental groups. The thesis is 20% growth through multi-site expansion. We have management data but no customer interviews yet.

## Sample output

### Provisional answer

Continue diligence, but do not underwrite 20% growth until site-level retention, group rollout behavior, and implementation capacity are validated. The most decision-sensitive question is whether signed groups convert additional sites fast enough to offset mature-site saturation.

### Critical hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Minimum evidence | Status | Deal implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-site rollout sustains growth | Cohorts by group, contracted sites, activated sites, and time to rollout | Missing | Could reduce growth case and valuation |
| Product is embedded enough to retain | GRR/NRR by cohort plus 8–10 customer interviews | Directional | Determines downside resilience |
| Implementation can support growth | Backlog, time-to-live, staffing, and churn during onboarding | Missing | May require investment and slower plan |
| Pricing has headroom | Discount history, renewal uplift, win/loss interviews | Unconfirmed | Potential upside, not base case |

### Ten-day workplan

- Days 0–2: reconcile recurring revenue, sites, customers, and cohort definitions; issue data request.
- Days 2–6: analyze cohorts and pipeline; complete 10 customers, 5 lost prospects, and 4 expert calls.
- Days 5–8: test competitors, pricing, implementation capacity, and downside scenarios.
- Days 8–10: synthesize IC memo, valuation sensitivities, red flags, and post-close priorities.

### Kill criteria

- Mature-cohort gross retention below 85% without a fixable cause.
- More than 30% of forecast growth depends on unsigned sites inside existing groups.
- Implementation capacity cannot support the base plan within the required investment envelope.
- Revenue or site definitions cannot be reconciled to billing data.

### IC decision gate

Revisit price if normalized growth falls below 12%, if net retention is below 100%, or if incremental implementation investment reduces the target return below the fund hurdle.

## Why this is a strong response

- It begins with the investment decision and uncertainties, not a generic market overview.
- It separates management assertions from evidence.
- It makes explicit what changes valuation or stops the deal.
