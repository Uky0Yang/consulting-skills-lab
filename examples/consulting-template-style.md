# Consulting Template Style — Worked Example

## User prompt

> Use $consulting-template-style to redesign a five-slide market-entry deck. The audience is an investment committee. Keep it brand-neutral, sparse, and evidence-led.

## Sample output

### Selected visual system

- Style: restrained, evidence-dense executive review.
- Canvas: 16:9, 32 px outer margin, consistent 12-column grid.
- Typography: Arial or Aptos; 26–30 pt action titles, 12–15 pt body, 9–10 pt sources.
- Color: charcoal text, warm gray structure, one muted blue for the recommended answer, amber only for material risk.

### Page map

| Slide | Action title | Layout | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Enter only through regulated mid-market buyers, where pain and willingness to pay align | Recommendation with three proof points | Segment economics and buyer interviews |
| 2 | The £180–230m serviceable pool is concentrated in two buyer segments | Segmented market bridge | Bottom-up sizing and cross-check |
| 3 | Incumbents own distribution, but workflow depth remains weak | Competitor matrix | Product review and win/loss signals |
| 4 | A compliance-led wedge beats a broad feature launch | Option comparison | Access, differentiation, economics, risk |
| 5 | Approve a gated £1.2m validation phase before full entry | 90-day roadmap and decision gates | Milestones, owners, stop criteria |

### Slide 2 specification

- Lead with the £180–230m answer in the title, not “Market size.”
- Use a horizontal segment bridge; label values directly and remove the legend.
- Put assumptions in a narrow right rail with confidence labels.
- Reserve blue for the two target segments; render excluded segments in gray.
- Add source/date beneath the chart and a one-line implication above it.

### QA gate

- Every title states a conclusion.
- All chart totals reconcile and units are visible.
- No element depends on color alone.
- Sources are readable at presentation size.
- The recommendation, economics, and risks use the same definitions across slides.

## Why this is a strong response

- It translates the decision story into page architecture before formatting.
- It uses style as hierarchy, not decoration.
- It remains brand-neutral and does not imitate proprietary templates.
